Jung's most extensive discussion of the parallels between psychology
and physics occurred in the supplement of the revised version of his essay,
"On the Nature of the Psyche." There he noted the corresponding problem
in physics and psychology of the investigation of non-perceptual features.
Whereas physics applies statistical laws to processes of atomic magnitude,
psychology pursues "the conscious processes until they lose themselves in
darkness and unintelligibility, and nothing more can be seen but effects which
have an organizing influence on the contents of consciousness" (para. 439). 4
