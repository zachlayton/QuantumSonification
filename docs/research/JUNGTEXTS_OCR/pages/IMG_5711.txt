In his essay, "Scientific and Epistemological Aspects of Ideas of the Un-
conscious," Pauli discussed the parallels between complementarity in physics
and the conscious / unconscious complementarity in psychology. He began
with James parallel between the concepts of field and subconscious and ex-
plored the problem inherent in each discipline wherein the act of observa-
tion cannot be separated from the properties of what is observed. Using the
example of observing the unconscious through dreams, Pauli noted that the
problem of observation in relation to the unconscious is more complicated
than in relation to the properties of physics':
The pure perception of a dream, if one may say so, has
already changed the state of consciousness and thereby created
a new phenomenon analogous to measuring observation in quan-
tum physics. The conscious reflection of a dream then has to have
a more extensive effect on the unconscious as a result, for which
there are no more direct analogies in physics. (p. 3)
