Above all, the feature of quantum mechanics highlighted by the EPR
experiment is the inherent non-locality of quantum phenomena. Non-local
behavior appears to lie at the heart of the mysterious nature of quantum reality.
Physicist N. David Mermin has observed that "the EPR experiment is as close
to magic as any physical phenomenon I know of..." 19 Non-locality implies
a type of behavior that remains outside the limits of comprehension set by
the classical scientific world-view of Western culture. When considered from
within the confines of that world-view, it does appear to be magical. It is
remarkable that the potentiality for non-local behavior in the physical world
had already been anticipated by Jung when he discussed acausal connections
as "synchronicity in a wider sense" more than 40 years ago.
During the past decade a number of authors have drawn attention to
the EPR experiment and the relationship between the concept of non-locality
and synchronicity. The most extensive discussion of this relationship has been
given by Victor Mansfield and J. Marvin Spiegelman in an essay entitled,
"Quantum Mechanics and Jungian Psychology: Building a Bridge." The
authors attempted to capture the essence of the EPR experiment by develop-
ing a non-technical, psychological analogy which they called "the parable of
the devoted twins." Following their discussion of the EPR experiment,
