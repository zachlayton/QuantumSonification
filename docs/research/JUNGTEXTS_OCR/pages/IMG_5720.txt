Because the original EPR experiment was only a thought-experiment
and not then well enough defined to be physically performed, it was con-
sidered to be a logical quandary and was often referred to as the "EPR
paradox." In 1951 a significant advance toward a practical physical experiment
was achieved by David Bohm with the publication of his version of the EPR
experiment. 1 In this simplified thought-experiment, which used spin states
instead of measurements of position and momentum, a system consisting of
two particles is prepared to have a total spin state of zero. From the symmetry
of the experimental arrangement and the conservation requirement that the
combined spins of both particles remain the same before and after their separa-
tion, the spin component of the second particle can be directly inferred to
be the negative of the first. This occurs because, in a zero spin state, the paired
particles must have equal and opposite spins that cancel each other.
The direction in which the spin is measured is chosen by the experi-
menter, who may select the measured orientation at random in such a way
that no local connection can be held to exist between the two particles. Never-
theless, in the outcome of the experiment, the spin of the second particle is
correlated with the randomly chosen direction of the spin measurement of
the first particle.
This result suggested that if the possibility of non-local connections was
denied, in accordance with the widely accepted postulates of special relativity,
then the ability to determine the spin of the second particle in any randomly
chosen direction could only be understood if that particle had an objectively
existing spin in that randomly chosen direction. Quantum mechanics, how-
ever, asserts that only one of three possible components of a particles spin
may be determined, while the other two components are necessarily indeter-
minable. Thus, the quantum mechanical description of a single particle was
again found to be incomplete.
In 1957 Bohm and Aharonov published another version of the EPR
experiment in which the polarization of correlated pairs of photons produced
in a positron-electron annihilation was considered. 15 This thought-experiment
was yet a further refinement of the basic EPR experiment with the intent to
move it towards a practical experimental test.
