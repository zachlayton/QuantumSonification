Anthony Stevens book, Archetypes: A Natural History of the Self, * is the
first wide-scale attempt to explore the biological evidence which supports the
validity of the archetype concept. He specifically approaches the archetype
concept as a scientific hypothesis capable of verification. To illustrate various
archetypal patterns of behavior, Stevens does not focus upon the content of
dreams, myths, and fantasies, but upon the work of ethologists and socio-
biologists with topics ranging from aggression to initiation rituals. He also
reviews the work of Rossi and Henry, as well as that of MacLean, Flor-Henry,
Jouvet, and others in an attempt to provide a synthesis of neurobiological find-
ings with Jungian concepts.
