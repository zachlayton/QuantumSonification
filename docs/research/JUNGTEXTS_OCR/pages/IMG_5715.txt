However, the archetype is conceived as more than an abstraction, and strictly
speaking, it is irrepresentable. Realizations of groups may include groups of
numbers, of matrices, of functions, etc. Therefore, the symmetries described
by groups may be physical symmetries such as the symmetries of crystals,
or they may be the abstract symmetries of various mathematical structures.
Physics uses group theory to describe various types of symmetries of
physical objects, but more importantly, it uses group theory in the formula-
tion of fundamental physical laws. Statements of symmetry are essentially state-
ments about the invariance of a particular property or quantity, and, in turn, the
invariance of a quantity is a statement of the conservation of that quantity. Thus,
principles of invariance and laws of conservation may be expressed using the
mathematics of groups. For example, invariance under linear spatial displace-
ment, rotational displacement, and temporal displacement implies the con-
servation of linear momentum, angular momentum, and energy, respectively.
Thus, in both classical and quantum mechanics, groups may be used to for-
mulate these fundamental conservation principles. In particle physics, group
theory is used to describe the conservation of more abstract quantities such
as electric charge, baryon number, lepton numbers, isospin, and hypercharge,
without which it would be impossible to understand the behavior of elemen-
tary particles.
