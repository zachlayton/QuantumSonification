During the 1970s the first experimental tests of Bell's inequality became
possible but were hampered by technical limitations. Then, between 1980 and
1982, the reports of three EPR experiments were published by Alain Aspect
and his associates at the Institut d'Optique Théoretique Appliqueé in Orsay,
France. " The results of these technically sophisticated experiments violated
Bell's inequality and demonstrated an impressive agreement with the predic-
tions of quantum mechanics. Although Aspect has admitted that their experi-
ments "have still some imperfections that leave some loop-holes open for the
advocates of hidden variable theories obeying Einstein's [local] causality," 18
their results generally have been accepted by physicists as indicating the cor-
rectness of quantum mechanics and diminishing the plausibility of an
objective-local explanation of quantum phenomena.
